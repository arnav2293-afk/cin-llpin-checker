How a CIN is built

Every CIN has 21 characters in six parts. Here’s a real one.

L or UListed on a stock exchange, or unlisted
State codeTwo letters, e.g. MH, KA, DL, TN
Company typePLC public, PTC private, OPC one person, GOI government

How to read a CIN

A CIN (Corporate Identity Number) is 21 characters. Each part means something:

Company type codes (characters 13–15)

State codes (characters 7–8)

LLPIN

An LLPIN (LLP Identification Number) is 3 letters, a hyphen and 4 digits, like AAB-1234. It doesn’t include listing, state or year.

Warnings (amber)

Some CINs have a valid format but a combination that company law doesn’t allow. They stay valid, with a warning to check:

  • Listed private company. A CIN starting with L whose type is OPC, PTC, GAP or ULT. Private companies can’t invite the public to buy their shares, so they can’t be listed (Companies Act 2013, s.2(68)).
  • One-person company before 2014. An OPC dated before 2014. OPCs could only be formed from 1 April 2014, when s.3 of the Companies Act 2013 came into force.
